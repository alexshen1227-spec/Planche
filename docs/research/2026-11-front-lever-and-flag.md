# Front lever and human flag — source-backed research

Compiled 2026-11-17/18. Written to match the tier discipline of
`docs/research-ledger.md` (STRONG · MODERATE · LIMITED · CONSENSUS · INFERENCE · OPEN).

## How to read this file

**Tiers.** CONSENSUS is used only where two or more independent *named* coaches
agree. One coach's number is labelled "single coach". Anything from an
unattributed page, a vendor page or a content farm is labelled as such and is
**not** CONSENSUS.

**How each source was seen** (this matters as much as the tier):

| **[FETCHED]** | The page was opened by the fetch tool and extracted by its summarising model. Numbers are as it reported them; I did not re-read the page line by line |
| **[SNIPPET]** | Seen only in a search-result summary. Treat as **UNVERIFIED** |
| **[BLOCKED]** | Could not be opened (status noted in the access log at the end) |

## Headline findings

1. **No study of the front lever hold or the human flag hold was found in
   PubMed.** Title/abstract phrase searches return only unrelated papers
   (myosin, mouse behaviour, steel clamps; FLAG-tagged proteins). The closest
   evidence is one self-report injury survey of 93 adult street-workout
   practitioners. Every ladder, bar and timeline below is coaching practice.
2. **Coaches disagree on the middle of the front lever ladder.** Steven Low's
   chart puts *straddle below half-lay/one-leg*; Antranik and Cali Move put
   *one-leg below straddle*; GMB and Maples have no one-leg step; Logan
   Christopher has no straddle step. Tuck → advanced tuck → … → full is the only
   part everyone shares.
3. **Hold-time bars spread about 12×** (5 s to 60 s) and two of the most
   credible sources (Low; Sommer's steady-state cycle) set **no gate at all** —
   they prescribe a fraction of a tested max and about a minute of total hold.
4. **The human flag is much thinner than the front lever.** Named-coach
   numbers that could be opened: clutch flag 20 s (Al Kavadlo), vertical flag
   20–30 s (Antranik, crediting Paul Scott). No named coach's full-flag
   standard could be verified. Low's chart gives an order (tuck → advanced tuck
   → straddle → full) and rates the full flag the same level as the full front lever.
5. **Safety evidence is survey-grade.** Tendinopathy and the shoulder lead the
   one survey; 10.3% of injured respondents named the front lever as what they
   were doing. That is not an injury rate. Nothing youth-specific was found.

**What everyone shares (CONSENSUS):** tuck first, advanced/flat/open tuck
second, full last; the lever is lengthened in stages.

**Where credible coaches disagree (OPEN):**

- **Straddle vs one-leg/half-lay order.** Low: straddle (L6) *before* half-lay/one-leg (L7).
  Antranik and Cali Move: one-leg *before* straddle. GMB/Maples (and FitnessFAQs, unverified):
  straddle only. Christopher: one-leg only.
  Low's chart orders the back lever and planche the same way (straddle below half-lay/one-leg).
- **Negatives first or holds first.** GMB insists on owning the slow lower into
  each shape before pausing in it. Low and Sommer programme holds as the main
  work (Low gives an eccentric conversion, see C).
- **Are dynamic pulls rungs or accessories?** Maples makes arch pulls, ice cream
  makers and front lever pulls *gates*. Low keeps "FL Rows" as a separate column.

**The honest spread:** 5 s (unattributed) → 15 s × 5 sets (Maples) → 15 s + 3–5 s
(Sommer 2008) → 60 s (Sommer 2004). Low and the steady-state cycle decline to set
a bar. Same pattern the ledger found for the planche: the "60 vs 15" split is one
coach at two dates.

**Competition standard (verified):** in the FIG men's Code of Points 2022–2024,
still rings, Element Group II "strength elements and hold elements (2 sec.)",
element 13 is the hanging scale frontways (front lever), held 2 s, in the
**A (0.1)** column; the back lever (element 7) is also A; the planche is C and
the straddled planche A, as the ledger already records. Read from the text
layer of the PDF, p. 86: https://abgym.ab.ca/app/uploads/2023/06/MAG-CoP-2022-2024.pdf
[READ]. The column was read from the element numbering under the A–E header.
The 2025–2028 code was not checked. So every training bar above is 2.5–30×
the sport's own 2 s — the same asymmetry the ledger notes for the planche.

## A8. Mechanics — load fraction per progression

**No sourced torque or "% of full" figure was found.** GMB gives only the
qualitative account: the shoulder is the axis, and the further the feet are from
it the harder the hold — taller is harder [READ]. The Santo Tomás document may
contain a force figure but is unverified.

What can be said from geometry (INFERENCE, no numbers attached):

- Shoulder torque = weight of everything on the far side of the shoulder × the horizontal distance of its centre of mass from the shoulder.
- Tucking shortens that distance through hip and knee angle, so — as with the planche (ledger §10) — **the joint angles are the dial and the step name is a label**. An "advanced tuck" covers a range.
- Abducting the legs by an angle θ each scales the legs' lever beyond the hip by cos θ. A narrow straddle is close to a full lever; a wide one is much easier. That alone can explain why coaches order straddle and one-leg differently.
- One leg extended with the other tucked sits between the advanced tuck and the full lever.

If the app wants fractions, derive them in the same model as `lib/loading.ts`
and label them a model (ledger "must not claim" #10).

**Shared (CONSENSUS, thin):** start near vertical, where the lever is short, and
bring the body down toward horizontal; shorten the legs (tuck / one leg / bent
knees) before the full shape; train both sides.

**How hard relative to the front lever (coaches differ):** Low's chart rates
the full flag and the full front lever the same level (8). Cali Move says the
front lever is harder than the flag for most people because it leans on pulling
strength, lists both at its level 4, and names no flag progression or hold time
(https://blog.calimove.com/2024/02/23/8-calisthenics-moves-you-should-try/ and
https://blog.calimove.com/2024/05/24/from-beginner-to-superhuman-calisthenics-levels-explained/, [FETCHED]).
A "hanging side pull" as Cali Move's main flag accessory was seen only in a
search summary — **UNVERIFIED**.

**Disagreement (OPEN):** clutch flag as a preliminary skill (Kavadlo only);
straddle as the last step before full (Low; FitnessVolt) versus one-leg/bent-knee
(Kavadlo; Antranik); entering top-down from vertical versus pressing up from the
floor (the anonymous copy calls bottom-up harder). Chamber holds, band
assistance and side-lever/side-plank prerequisites: **not found in any opened
named-coach source**.

# Recommended ladder + bars

These are **suggestions that sit inside the coaching spread**, not standards.
"Start hold" follows the app's existing opening-dose idea (Sommer 2008's 3–5 s on
the next variation). Bars shorten as the lever lengthens, as the ledger's planche
reconciliation does. Copy must say the bars are convention.

## Front lever

| Step | Key exercise | Suggested start hold | Suggested unlock hold | Tier | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Base (gate, not a rung) | Active hang, scapular pulls, skin the cat / inverted hang, hollow hold; 5 strict pull-ups | — | 30 s inverted or tucked hang; 5 pull-ups | Single coach | Maples; Low chart L1–3 |
| 1. Tuck | Tuck front lever, back rounded | 5 s | 20 s | CONSENSUS on the step; bar is design (15 s × 5 Maples; 15 s Sommer 2008; 60–70% of max Low) | Low L4; GMB; Antranik; Maples; Christopher |
| 2. Advanced (flat/open) tuck | Flat back, knees past the elbows | 4–5 s | 15 s | CONSENSUS on the step; bar is design | Low L5; GMB; Antranik; Maples; Christopher |
| 3. One leg / half-lay | One leg straight, other tucked; alternate sides, credit the weaker | 3–4 s | 12 s each side | **OPEN — order contested** (Low puts this *after* the straddle; Antranik and Cali Move before; GMB and Maples omit it) | Antranik; Cali Move; Christopher; Low L7 |
| 4. Straddle | Legs straight and apart, hips open | 3–4 s | 10 s | **OPEN — order contested**; hip pike is the known fault here | Low L6; GMB; Maples; Antranik |
| 5. Full | Legs together | 3 s | Goal: 5–10 s | CONSENSUS on the step; Maples's ten-second ceiling is the only named figure | Low L8; all |

Pull version per step (trailing the hold, as the planche press does): tuck rows
once the advanced tuck is being held; advanced tuck rows at the straddle/one-leg
stage; straddle rows around the full lever (Low chart: rows sit one to two levels
above the same-shape hold). Negatives into each new shape before pausing in it (GMB).

Choice the app has to make and say out loud: steps 3 and 4 can be offered in
either order or as alternatives. A side-view camera sees a one-leg lever far
better than a straddle (INFERENCE), which is a product reason, not a coaching one.

# Must not claim

1. **Any front lever or flag hold-time bar as validated, standard or "the rule".** The spread is 5–60 s and two leading sources set no bar.
2. **"Research/EMG shows…" about either hold.** No study of either was found in PubMed. Say that; do not say "there is no research" (Google Scholar and SPORTDiscus were not searched, and unread candidates exist).
3. **A timeline or completion date** for any step or either skill, including "tuck in 2–3 months" and "flag in 7 weeks".
4. **An injury rate or risk for either skill.** Ngo 2021's 10.3% is the share of injured survey respondents who were doing a front lever — self-report, adults only, no exposure data.
5. **That the human flag is a shown shoulder risk.** The survey only hypothesises it in its introduction.
6. **That any grip, scapular position, warm-up or "train both sides" prevents injury.**
7. **"Low requires the back lever first."** Unverified; only the chart's level order is known.
8. **A settled order for straddle versus one-leg/half-lay.** Named coaches differ.
9. **Retracted-and-depressed as the only correct scapula, or neutral as a fault.** Two coaches cue retraction; the question was not resolved.
10. **Any torque or "% of a full lever" figure as measured.** None was sourced; a model must be called a model.
11. **A pull-up, push-up or handstand push-up count as "the prerequisite".** Sources say 5, 8, 10 and "lots".
12. **The clutch flag as required** for the press flag (one coach), or a required grip width (no named coach gives one).
13. **Band colour as a share of bodyweight removed** (ledger: Uchida 2016).
14. **The FIG 2 s as a training standard, or as the current rule.** What is verified: the 2022–2024 men's code lists the rings front lever as an A-value hold of 2 s. The 2025–2028 code was not checked.
15. **Anything youth-specific** as evidence-based. No youth data was found.
16. **The unattributed numbers** (FitnessVolt's pass marks, "15–20 s per step", the anonymous flag blog's 30 s rules) as coaching consensus.
17. **The GMB/Kavadlo flag numbers or Danny Kavadlo's 5 s** as quotations — the pages could not be opened.
18. **"Connective tissue adapts more slowly than muscle" or "a warm-up protects the shoulder"** — a coach's line (Cali Move) that the ledger already rules out (rules 4 and 7).
19. **"6–12 months to a front lever"** or any other coach's estimate as an expectation.
20. **The pectoralis rupture case report as a front lever injury** — it happened in a different move.
21. **That the front lever is harder (or easier) than the human flag.** Low's chart rates them equal; Cali Move says the lever is harder for most; nobody measured it.
